AI and Crypto are moving closer together, but truly usable AI + Crypto products remain scarce.

AI requires compute, data, models, and application scenarios. Crypto provides open data, global liquidity, fast event reaction, community coordination, and programmable economic mechanisms. The combination can create new intelligent applications, but the market still has a clear gap between narrative and real usable products.

Most AI + Web3 projects today cluster around three categories. The first category is compute resource networks, where the competition is GPU supply, pricing, and scheduling efficiency. The second category is general AI Agent or agent-tokenization platforms, where the competition is agent creation, issuance, and coordination. The third category is data, model, or incentive networks, where the competition is open contribution and network incentives.

DI chooses a different battlefield: crypto market intelligence. Compute, data, models, and agents are production inputs for a larger goal: delivering usable crypto market intelligence. DI uses real compute as the execution substrate, the 800TB crypto-native model as the market cognition layer, Agent Network as the task coordination layer, and Event Engine, AI Quant Intelligence, Compute Packs, API Services, and Enterprise Intelligence as product entrances.

## Information Overload

Crypto markets have extremely high information density.

Users need to process news, on-chain data, exchange data, project announcements, macro data, regulatory updates, social sentiment, security events, fund flows, and KOL narratives every day. More information does not automatically create better intelligence. It often makes judgment more fragmented.

Users need a system that can turn complex events into structured tasks and continuously track their impact.

## Fragmented Data

On-chain data, market data, social data, project data, macro data, and historical event data are often distributed across different platforms.

A stablecoin risk event may involve on-chain fund migration, exchange liquidity, social panic, derivatives leverage, market-making depth, and regulatory signals at the same time. Without unified cross-source understanding, users struggle to form a complete view.

## AI Tools Lack Crypto-Native Context

General AI models can process text and reasoning tasks, but they have limited native understanding of crypto market structure.

Crypto contains specialized contexts: wallet behavior, protocol mechanisms, token economics, exchange structures, stablecoin systems, derivatives leverage, MEV, DeFi composability, community narratives, project life cycles, and regulatory paths. Without continuous training and structured domain data, it is difficult to produce high-quality outputs in real market scenarios.

## High Compute Access Barriers

AI compute is often delivered through cloud resources, enterprise services, long-term rental contracts, or complex configuration. For individual researchers, developers, small teams, and early Web3 projects, direct access to high-quality compute remains difficult.

DI turns real compute capacity into accessible product and service units. For ordinary users, compute is embedded inside Event Engine, AI Quant Intelligence, and enterprise services. For developers and advanced users, Compute Packs divide compute into small, purchasable, executable, and consumable service quotas.

## Event-Driven Markets Need Event Intelligence

Crypto markets are highly event-driven. War, regulation, exchange risk, stablecoin risk, security incidents, macro shocks, and narrative shifts can rapidly affect asset prices, liquidity, risk appetite, and on-chain behavior.

The market needs more than news aggregation. It needs a system that can identify events, break down variables, simulate transmission paths, organize agents, output multiple scenarios, and continuously calibrate after events unfold.
